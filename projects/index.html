Advanced Zeolite Catalysts for
Ultra-Low NOx Emissions

Designed and synthesized next-generation copper-exchanged zeolite catalysts achieving 94% NOx conversion efficiency at real-world diesel exhaust conditions—surpassing EPA Tier 4 standards ahead of schedule.

Challenge & Approach

Diesel engines remain critical across heavy industry, yet NOx emissions pose severe public health risks. Existing commercial catalysts degraded rapidly above 650 °C, limiting their effectiveness in real-world high-temperature cycles. The objective was to engineer thermally robust active sites with superior ammonia storage capacity without sacrificing selectivity.

Leveraging a novel two-step ion-exchange protocol combined with hydrothermal aging simulation, the synthesized Cu-SSZ-13 variants demonstrated unprecedented stability. Synchrotron XRD and in-situ DRIFTS spectroscopy were employed to map active-site evolution under realistic operating conditions.

Catalysis · Oak Ridge National Laboratory
Advanced Zeolite Catalysts for Ultra-Low NOx Emissions

Background & Motivation

Stringent EPA Tier 4 emissions regulations demand >90% NOx reduction across a wide operating window. Commercial Cu-SSZ-13 catalysts lose activity rapidly above 650 °C due to Cu migration and zeolite dealumination—a critical limitation for turbocharged diesel applications.

Technical Innovation

A novel two-step ion-exchange protocol coupled with controlled steaming introduced a population of isolated Cu²⁺ sites anchored to paired Al sites, dramatically reducing Cu mobility at high temperature. Simultaneous doping with trace La³⁺ stabilized the zeolite framework against hydrothermal dealumination.

Characterization Methods

Synchrotron pair distribution function (PDF) analysis, in-situ DRIFTS during NH₃-SCR cycles, temperature-programmed reduction (TPR), and N₂ physisorption were employed to quantify structural changes across the catalyst lifecycle.

Industrial Impact

The catalyst formulation was licensed by [Industry Partner] for integration into their next-generation selective catalytic reduction (SCR) aftertreatment systems. Projected to reduce NOx output equivalent to removing 50,000 diesel trucks from the road annually.

Energy Materials · Argonne National Lab
Silicon-Anode SEI Engineering for Next-Gen Li-Ion Batteries

Problem Statement

Silicon anodes offer 10× higher theoretical capacity than graphite but suffer from massive volume changes (up to 300%) during cycling, causing SEI fracture, capacity fade, and safety concerns under fast-charge protocols critical for EV adoption.

Approach

Screened a library of 24 electrolyte additive combinations using design-of-experiments methodology. Fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC) combined with a novel bis(trimethylsilyl) phosphate additive produced a mechanically compliant SEI with superior ionic conductivity.

Results

3× cycle life extension at 3C fast-charge rate; 15% improvement in coulombic efficiency. Published in ACS Catalysis (IF 13.1) with 42 citations in 18 months, establishing a widely-adopted electrolyte screening framework.

Process Development · Pilot Scale
Continuous Hydrothermal Synthesis of Functional Oxides

Scale-Up Challenge

Batch hydrothermal synthesis produces heterogeneous particle size distributions and requires long cycle times, making commercial production cost-prohibitive. A continuous flow process was essential to achieve the uniformity and throughput required by photovoltaic manufacturers.

Engineering Solution

Designed a plug-flow tubular reactor with precise temperature zoning (±2 °C), a back-pressure regulator system for sub- and supercritical water operation, and an inline dynamic light scattering sensor enabling real-time particle size feedback control.

Outcomes

10 kg/day phase-pure anatase TiO₂ at ±3% particle size distribution (12 nm target); 60% manufacturing cost reduction versus batch; IP position under evaluation for commercialization partnership.

Sustainability · DOE-Funded
CO₂ Capture & Conversion via Bifunctional MOF Sorbents

Vision

Existing carbon capture systems require separate and energy-intensive regeneration steps before CO₂ can be utilized. This project integrates capture and catalytic conversion into a single material platform, dramatically simplifying process architecture.

Materials Design

HKUST-1 MOF scaffolds were post-synthetically modified with primary amine groups for CO₂ chemisorption and impregnated with Ru nanoparticles for catalytic CO₂ hydrogenation to methanol—all within a single bifunctional sorbent-catalyst composite.

Current Status

Laboratory validation complete with 98% CO₂ selectivity from simulated flue gas (15% CO₂ / N₂ balance) and demonstrated CO₂-to-methanol conversion at 150 °C. Scale-up design phase initiated; manuscript in preparation.

Thermally Stable Bimetallic Pd–Pt/Al₂O₃ Oxidation Catalysts

Technical Challenge

Automotive diesel oxidation catalysts (DOC) must maintain >95% CO and hydrocarbon (HC) oxidation efficiency after 100,000-mile service. At high temperatures, Pd and Pt sinter into large, low-activity particles—a core durability failure mode.

Patented Innovation

Engineered strong metal-support interactions (SMSI) through a calcination-reduction-passivation protocol that anchors Pd–Pt alloy nanoparticles to specific alumina surface sites, preventing sintering up to 900 °C. The bifunctional bimetallic composition also reduces Pd loading by 40% vs. commercial standards.
